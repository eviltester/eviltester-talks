This talk was performed at the British Computer Society Special Interest Group in Software Testing in March 2016.

This talk adds 'models of testing' to the Systems of Development space, where we can view testing 'as feedback' or 'as learning'. To help us re-model the testing process.

Systems used as examples are RestMud and The House Of Test Data Generator.

At the time, RestMud was not available for download, but it is now, so you can find it on my compendiumdev.co.uk web site - and there are links to it in the exercises and resources pdfs.

Also discuss precondition analysis and presupposition analysis in a testing context. And additional discussion on how adding tools or adjusting process has to be evaluated for risk.

Also includes an overview of testing from technical information compared to security testing. With some practical 'instant win' steps for getting started with technical testing.

When I listened back to the live talk, I think it has a lot of energy and covered a lot of information quite succinctly and there are also topics in there that I hope encourage you to research in more detail.

Its also the first time that I've discussed presupposition and precondition analysis for about 10 years and I do think those are important topics.

Enjoy.

I think this is a pretty good overview talk and I mention some important points in here that I don't think I had covered before. Particularly the 'as if' modelling of testing. Because I think people get hung up on 'testing is' and 'testing is just' or 'testing is only' when in fact that is not true. Testing is many things to many people, and probably all of them are valid.

So the reality is that we need the ability to hole many, and possibly conflicting, models of testing in our head at the same time. The  'as if' modelling might help you do that. Certainly I have found that useful and taking a single model i.e. thinking of 'testing as a feedback process' has helped me gain some insights into my processes and communication approaches.

There is a lot of bedrock mentioned in here, so I do encourage you to follow up on the exercises and the resources mentioned. I know there is a lot of stuff, but we have a big subject that we are studying. Testing has been around for millions of years, way before programming, so don't expect to learn it all in a couple of years.

If you start to use presupposition analysis and precondition analysis in your testing, you will be building models as you test and I think it is a fairly important 'secret' that underpins my approach. It is also very important when dealing with people and trying to understand their models of the world to help improve your ability to communicate on a one to one basis.
