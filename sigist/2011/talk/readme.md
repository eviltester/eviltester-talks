## Learning, Developing, Evolving: The Path of the Technical Tester

Alan Richardson's talk at the BCS SIGIST conference on 16 March 2011 at the Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists in London.

The talk explores learning technical skills through systems and models, observing and manipulating applications, and deriving testing approaches from first principles. A worked web-application example expands from a form into browsers, servers, architecture, and users.

More details:

https://www.eviltester.com/conference/sigist2011_conference/

## Conversion

All 23 slides are retained in source order. The PDF was generated from the original PowerPoint using LibreOffice; no full slide-deck PDF was present in the source folder. It retains the original white-background design and artwork.

The Markdown keeps editable slide text, nested lists, and source links. Slides 15-19 use rendered images alongside their titles to preserve the placement of screenshots within the hand-drawn diagrams. The 55 embedded speaker-note paragraphs are retained with their slide numbers.

The complete 304-node mind map is converted to a hierarchical outline, including labels stored as rich text. The separate working document is split into 274 paragraphs of preparation notes and 127 paragraphs of prepared talk narrative. Its earlier working title and alternative sections are retained. The copied email discussion and eight submission-deadline entries are omitted.

The draft paper retains all 220 content paragraphs, its web-form image, and its HTML example as a fenced code block. The generated table of contents is omitted. The source has unfinished sections and placeholders, which are retained and identified as draft material.

These notes, script, outline, and paper are preparation materials, not transcripts of the delivered talk.

Only the 18 images referenced by Markdown are included: 13 native images and five rendered slide compositions. Original presentation, document, and mind-map binaries, unused loose images, individual artwork PDF exports, thumbnail caches, and editor files remain in the source archive.
