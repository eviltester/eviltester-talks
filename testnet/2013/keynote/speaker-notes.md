## Slide 8 - I started my career building silver bullets

In terms of my background I was essentially built to quest for Silver Bullets. I started my career in CASE tools, Computer Aided Software Engineering tools, where we would 'draw' programs to avoid coding errors, I wrote one with a COBOL interpreter built in so that we could execute the diagrams rather than generate code. The company I worked for went bankrupt.

So before I even started in testing I was building Silver Bullets.

At the start of my quest I was building Silver Bullets for other people to sell.

I started working with a consultancy company, building Silver Bullets for them. This time a CAST (Computer Aided Software Testing) tool for SSADM. This was the clumsiest tool ever. The company I worked for went bankrupt.

I moved on to writing another CAST tool for them - this was in the days before TestDirector had really taken over the world so Test Process tools were in vogue. I was programming rather than designing the tool and though I questioned some of the design decisions they weren't changed. The company I worked for went bankrupt.

## Slide 10 - You can learn a lot by making Silver Bullets

Over this I learned a lot about modelling tools and processes. I learned to pursue the highest level of abstraction possible in our modelling, this has a knock on effect on test automation abstraction layers but I didn't see the relevance until later.
