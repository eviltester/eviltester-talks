## An NLP Session

Saying what NLP “is” doesn't really tell us anything.

If I provide a quick overview of an NLP Session then that might give a little better overview of NLP.

So an NLP Session would typically be used for some sort of 'therapeutic' reason. Some sort of 'therapy' type situation – cure a phobia, reduce pain. Something like that.

The basic approach is to find out what is going on, what the perceived problem is, etc. Build up some sort of context.

And then find out 'how' that the person knows that whatever it is is happening, and also the internal processes and strategies that cause that to happen or the reaction to happen.

So:

“I'm depressed”

“how do you know you're depressed”

“because I'm always sad”

“you don't look sad now”

“sometimes I'm less depressed”

“so if you're not always sad, how do you know when to be depressed?”

My wife did some training in NLP as well and she was speaking to someone who was off work with 'depression' and she asked that person a similar question. “how do you know you're depressed”, and the answer was “because I'm still on the happy pills from the doctor”. Which I thought was really sad but that happens, and at the same time I laughed, because we do get situations where the system propogates through 'bad' practices and effects and changes the beliefs about why those practices and effects are in place.

And NLP has a whole structure for dealing with those situations.

This is different from some therapeutic processes in that it is more about the 'how' and the strategies and mechanisms than a person uses than it is about the 'why', the reasons and justifications.

So there is a modeling process going on, which is based on a communication process which involves questioning, examining ambiguity in the statements that someone says, exploring what someone says with literalism can help emphasize ambiguity or generalizations.

And for me this is what a testing session is like.

I model systems, I want to figure out what is going on and how something is doing what it is doing and then I'll question it, question the assumptions that are inherent in the how. I'll take systems and requirements literally and press buttons when they are pressable even if they don't make sense.

And I may well explore some 'whys', “I wonder why that happened” but when I say that I really mean “I wonder how that happened” “I wonder what at this point in time resulted in that occurring”

Once I know the strategy and the method for causing a problem to occur in the system then the developer can figure out “why” that happened. That's not really what I work with. I work with the how. And when I present the defects to people, I'm presenting a 'how' situation – a series of observations and models, rather than a set of beliefs about why something happened.

## NLP Presuppositions

I put this slide in to kind of explain where NLP comes from.

Presuppositions are things that we take to be true for what follows to be true. And NLP these are just models that are built on. And we can see that they actually don't derive from NLP.

“The Map is not the territory” comes from General Semantics, and it is basically saying that our map, the stuff in our head, is not the real thing so we should deal as much as possible with the real thing and change our maps and models to allow us to deal better with the real world.

This is one of the key presuppositions for Rational Emotive Behaviour Therapy which deals with the musts/shoulds the modal operators of obligation and necessity helps people by changing their models of the world to use modal operators of possibility like 'could'.

“Life and Mind are Systemic Processes” comes from cybernetics and basically says that people are systems, what we think affects our physiology and our physiology affects how we think and what we think and we can approach the study of ourselves systemically.

And then taking these presuppositions there are a whole bunch of derived or corollary statements that feed in to NLP as 2nd order presuppositions and 3rd order presuppositions etc. And these are part of the approach to NLP.

In NLP these is another use for presuppositions and that is part of the meta-model where given a particular statement or behaviour, we can ask ourselves “what has to be true for this to be true” and we can build up a model that way. I find this to be an enormously rich technique when applied to software testing for building up user models, targeting dialogs for exploratory testing, building up failure scenarios to test against.

One of the corollary statements which I'm going to look at is the Law of Requisite variety, which again is from Cybernetics.

I don't think I would really have studied much of the work in cybernetics, semantics, linguistics, psychology and psychotherapy if I hadn't started with NLP and those areas are rich mines of information that I use to analyse what I do. And NLP has used a lot of that work and generalized from it to create simple models and approaches. And that is one reason why I find it useful.

- Requirements had to be signed off

- Test Artifacts had to be in the test repository database

And sometimes this stuff is useful some of the time. But not ALL the time. I'm absolutely certain about that.

If I come in with a set of fixed beliefs, then that is what I am going to work within.

I'm not going to analyse the context of what is actually going on. Or if I do then I'm going to analyse it through my belief filters.

It is really hard to be contextual with a lot of fixed beliefs.

In NLP when working with someone we are trying to identify which beliefs are limiting, are restricting their choices, are decreasing their requisite variety.

We can do the same for testing.

I encourage you, implore you, plead with you, please god, analyse your testing beliefs.

If you identify any which act as limiting in the context that you find yourself testing then dumping them will improve your testing process immediately, instantly, and easily, and the only thing you change is yourself, and then your behaviour changes.

And if you find some beliefs that are present in your context that aren't yours then question them, challenge them, go around them.
