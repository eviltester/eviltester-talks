## Slide 1 - Open Source &amp; Free Test Tools

Background:

Used the big name commercial tools, used a lot of free and open source. I've even written commercial and open source tools a range of JSP diagrammer, Java automation library for windows, test and defect managers, test data tools, documentation tools etc.

I try to stay up to date with tools but that gets harder and harder every year sa more and more tools become available to us.

Some questions that might come up in this session that we might want to discuss. When is O/S better? How free is free? If you go O/S can you get the staff and the support? Whatever is on your mind – whatever questions you've had ask them of the group and we'll do our best to voice a range of answers.

In the introduction I aim to cover:

Why I care,

What I use – and why,

What I've noticed,

What I've heard,

Why I use what I use

Difficulties I've faced,

Future possibilities,

How I keep up to date

About this session: For the last 16 years, Alan Richardson has both programmed, and used, open Source and commercial tools. The session's introduction will summarise some of Alan's preferences, experiences and strategies. We hope the group will contain experienced users of open source tools, the curious but inexperienced, and detractors of O/S. We can then share experiences - both good and bad - with O/S tools compared with our commercial tool experiences. We can explore our motivations behind tool selection, going beyond cost comparisons. So please, come and share your experiences, and we can learn how to maximise the effectiveness of our tool choices.

## Slide 3 - Is O/S your 1st Choice?

If I have a testing problem that a tool will help with then my first choice is open source.

I want a toolkit of tools rather than a single one size all fit tool.

With an open source tool I can use the tool and solve the problem by only investing time.

I don't then feel that the tool has to justify its investment it has only to solve a problem.

Too many companies look for a single tool and then 'justify' their investment by making the bought tool the 'only' tool that people can use

What is your first choice and why?
